I used to be firmly against parental controls. It felt like spying, an invasion of privacy destined to backfire into a minefield of teenage rebellion. I pictured myself as the cool, trusted mom whose kid would want to talk to me. Then my daughter turned two, and everything got real, fast.

It wasn’t about catching her looking at bad stuff-it was about the sheer, soul-crushing volume of YouTube Kids videos she could swipe through before breakfast. It was about the tablet becoming a pacifier, a babysitter, a shortcut to peace and quiet that left me feeling hollow afterward. My “trusted” approach wasn’t building connection; it was just letting her drift in a digital sea while I tried to get a work email sent. The control I needed wasn't over her, but over our home's digital environment. So we started using simple timers and app filters. Not to spy, but to shape. To turn the endless buffet of content back into a curated meal. It's less about policing and more about pausing. It’s a guardrail on the information superhighway, one that gives us both a chance to look up from the screen and actually see each other.
