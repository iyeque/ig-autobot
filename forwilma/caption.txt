I used to think I needed an elaborate system to manage screens at home-complex timers, app controls, lengthy negotiations. Then my husband and I started fighting about it daily, and our 2-year-old was picking up on every tense word.

Last month, we tried something radical instead: one simple routine.

Every evening after dinner (around 7pm), we sit together for exactly ten minutes. My daughter plays with blocks while we check if she needs anything before bedtime-a hug? A song? Water? After that quick connection, we do a two-part wind-down:

First, all devices get set aside. Not hidden-we're honest adults can be on phones during downtime-but active scrolling stops.

Second, we each share one thing from our day using three words maximum. That constraint keeps it light. Today mine were "meeting/stressed/tired."

The shift has been remarkable. We argue maybe once monthly now versus nightly. Our daughter goes to sleep calmer because she felt seen firsthand, not just managed through routines.

Turns out what kids actually need isn't more control over their media-they need us present for actual moments. Even messy ones.
