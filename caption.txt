I once heard someone say that trees don’t just survive adversity-they thrive in it, growing stronger roots and more resilient trunks. It’s a beautiful metaphor, isn’t it? Or at least, it sounds lovely until you realize how much of that supposed "strength" is really just survival instinct dressed up as virtue.

In reality, the idea of “resilience” has been turned into one of the most cringeworthy buzzwords in human culture. It's become synonymous with gritting your teeth through trauma while pretending everything’s fine-like if being emotionally scarred was somehow admirable because you kept going. And now we’re told this kind of endurance makes us strong, even when that same resilience might be keeping us stuck in places where we should have left long ago.

Take the tree example again: sure, it grows deeper roots under pressure-but does that make it better? Does that mean its pain doesn’t matter anymore? Maybe we're not meant to endure suffering just so we can call ourselves survivors. We may be survivors, but let’s stop pretending we've grown taller or wiser from it.

There's something almost tragic about romanticizing what it means to bend without breaking. The whole concept feels like a hollow comfort trick meant for people who believe they must prove their worth by enduring humiliation, betrayal, loneliness, or abuse-in short, all those things life throws at us that are entirely outside our control.

But here's what I think: maybe growth doesn't come from surviving hard times-it comes from learning when to walk away. Not every root needs to dig deep; sometimes, it's enough to simply decide not to stay planted in toxic soil.
