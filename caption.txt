I've been thinking about how we treat our mistakes lately—and honestly? We're all walking around with cracked pottery pretending nothing's broken while secretly hoping nobody notices the glue.

But what if we did what they do in kintsugi? What if instead of hiding your failures with cheap ceramic paint, you highlighted them in gold?

My new essay "The Elegance of Flaws" isn't about toxic positivity or telling yourself "you're enough!" (though maybe you are). It's about something more interesting: recognizing that every scar tells a better story than perfect porcelain ever could.

Because here's the truth nobody wants to say—the most beautiful things aren't the ones that never broke. They're the ones someone took the trouble to fix, even when fixing wasn't necessary.
