I’ve spent a decade polishing a flawless façade—only to watch it shatter spectacularly.

Turns out “success” was never the destination; it was the excuse we used to weaponize perfection against our own messiness. In The Nine Stitches I argue that our obsession with being immaculate blinds us to the simple truth: we’re flawed, fleeting beings.

Embracing scars isn’t surrender; it’s the realization that the crack where light sneaks in (yes, a Leonard Cohen nod—because even cynics need a lyric) is precisely where growth sprouts.

Quit chasing an imagined, immaculate you. Start stitching the rag‑torn remnants after the collapse. That’s where the real, interesting work begins.
