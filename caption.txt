RIP:
Envision the impact of your actions, like ripples on a lake, affecting the world around us. How can we become more aware of the consequences of our choices?

Imagine every decision as a stone dropped into reality's still pond—not just one ripple, but concentric circles spreading outward until they hit someone else's shore. That "harmless" comment about your coworker's presentation style becomes their imposter syndrome during the big pitch. Your choice to drive instead of bike adds to traffic that makes your neighbor miss their daughter's recital because she couldn't get home from work on time.

Awareness isn't some mystical state achieved through meditation (though props if you've figured out how to sit comfortably for twenty minutes straight—I haven't). It's more like noticing when your usual coffee order now requires explaining why you don't want the plastic cup, or realizing the person behind you in line has been waiting longer than seems fair.

Being conscious of consequences means accepting that everything touches something else. You're not operating in isolation; you're just another node in this absurd network of cause-and-effect where someone always ends up holding the bag of someone else's carelessness.

So maybe start small. Notice when your convenience costs someone else effort. Acknowledge that your comfort might be built on someone's discomfort. And try, even just once, to throw the pebble gently.
