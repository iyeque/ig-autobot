The body is skin deep—but perception runs deeper.

That flimsy membrane between what we are and everything else isn’t a wall; it’s a suggestion. Your fingertips sense cold metal’s heat before they even touch. Your eyes fling light into meaning three feet ahead. Even taste— that intimate betrayal—happens across invisible membranes.

Skin marks where matter ends and experience begins. Where flesh becomes… whatever comes after.

But that “after” isn’t some mystical bullshit. It’s the gap between neuron spikes and conscious thought, between raw sensory data and the stories we spin about reality.

The boundary moves. Stand too close to someone uncomfortable and watch their bubble expand. Touch yourself versus touching another, and notice how context rewrites biology in an instant.

So I’ll ask you this: Where do you feel that boundary? Is it in your fingernails when you type alone at midnight? Does it shift during meditation—or while watching strangers argue online?

Tell me where yours lives. I’m pretty sure mine migrated somewhere around page seven of Chapter Two.
