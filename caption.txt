Ah, yes. Heraclitus's ancient whopper: panta rhei - everything flows. The Greek philosopher probably said it while watching a river, which sounds pretentious until you realize he was right about literally everything.

We spend our twenties mortgaging our futures to buy houses that will depreciate while we appreciate (ha!). We invest in careers with five-year plans, only to discover the world has a different timeline. We cling to relationships, certainties, versions of ourselves that have already dissolved into memory before we've even packed up the moving boxes.

"The only constant is change" sounds like a Hallmark card written by someone who just discovered Buddhism at a yoga retreat. But here's what they don't tell you: that phrase isn't meant to be comforting. It's a diagnosis.

Everything changes. Your body. Your bank account. Your opinions about that band you loved at seventeen. That relationship you thought would define your life? It did, and then it ended. The project that made your career? Obsolete three years later. The person you are today? Unrecognizable to the you from five years ago.

And yet. And yet.

Here’s where the cynicism cracks open just enough for something resembling wisdom to leak through: We don’t find strengt...
