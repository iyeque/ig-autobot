Ripples and Resonance #17

Drop a stone into still water and the ripples spread outward, touching shores you never intended to reach. The initial splash is local and brief, but the effects travel far beyond what the stone's size would suggest.

This is how actions ripple through systems. A decision made in one department echoes through another. A kindness shown to a stranger circles back through ways you'll never trace. A failure in one project teaches lessons that strengthen completely unrelated work years later.

The mistake we make is expecting the impact to match the intention. We think small actions should have small consequences, and large actions should have predictable large ones. But systems don't work that way. The most profound changes often start as something so small it barely registers, while the most carefully planned initiatives sometimes vanish without a trace.

Ripples and resonance: the stone doesn't choose where the ripples go. It only chooses to be thrown. The water decides the rest.

What do you think - are you more concerned with the stone you're holding, or the ripples you can't control?
