Imagine trying to fail and actually pulling it off. There’s a deliciously absurd charm in sweating over a disaster and nailing it.

It makes you sniff at every “growth mindset” seminar you’ve ever endured. Turns out failure isn’t always a teacher; sometimes it’s just a mirror. You’re not learning anything when you can’t even botch things properly.

Here’s the kicker: success and failure aren’t polar opposites—they’re just outcomes. Waste too much energy fretting over which label you’ll wear, and you’ll miss the point: the act of doing matters more than the sticker slapped on the result.

So perhaps intentional failure is simply the moment you finally admit you were never really in control.
