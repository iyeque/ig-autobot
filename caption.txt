We're obsessed with perfection because we've confused it with worthiness. We think if only our skin were clearer, our portfolios fatter, our relationships less complicated, our lives would finally matter. That somehow these buffed, flawless surfaces are what make us valuable.
What a load of beautiful, shiny bulljunk.
Your scars aren't just evidence you survived-they're proof that you fought when others folded. That crack in your confidence isn't weakness; it's where the light gets in (yes, I'm quoting Leonard Cohen through a Dylan lyric, deal with it). Your "flaws"-that messy handwriting, that awkward pause before you answer difficult questions, those weird little habits no one else has-those aren't bugs in your operating system. They're features. They're the fingerprints of a soul that hasn't been completely sanded down by convention.
Think about it: what makes someone magnetic isn't their Instagram feed. It's the contradiction between their confident stride and the way they nervously adjust their gljerkes. It's the pjerkion behind their eyes even when their words come out all wrong. Perfection is boring. Perfect people have nothing left to prove except how perfectly imperfect everyone else is.
