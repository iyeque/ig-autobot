Here we go again-another grand scheme to bend reality to our will, another heroic attempt to master what cannot be tamed: time itself. You want to control it? Please. That's like trying to wrestle a greased slippery eel made of liquid light while blindfolded and wearing mittens. Someone's going to get hurt, and it won't be time.

The Grand Illusion

Let me tell you about my brilliant plan last Tuesday. I had exactly forty-five minutes before an important meeting (not that it mattered-they were all equally soul-crushing anyway). Forty-five precious minutes! So I constructed this beautiful little schedule:

15 min - Respond to critical emails
20 min - Finish that report
8 min - Strategic coffee consumption
2 min - Breathing exercise to look zen-like
5 min - Bathroom mirror pep talk

Did any of this happen? Of course not. What actually happened was I spent seventeen minutes arguing with Amazon Alexa because she misunderstood "Alexa, play focus music" as "Alexa, play Frozen soundtrack," then ten minutes staring at a spreadsheet cell wondering if my life had become so meaningless that Excel formulas now held more emotional resonance than actual human relationships, followed by accidentally microwaving my phone charger thinking it was my lunch.

Time didn't care about my intentions. It just kept... happening. Tick-tock, tick-tock, laughing its invisible face at me.
