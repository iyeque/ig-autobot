Freedom isn't the opposite of constraint; they're more like two sides of a Möbius strip wearing matching friendship bracelets.

Think about it: every constraint creates possibility through elimination. Like how haiku's rigid structure unlocks infinite emotional specificity, or how a sonnet's architecture holds entire universes within fourteen lines. Constraints don't cage creativity—they give it something specific to push against.

True innovation rarely comes from unlimited resources ("I'll just add water!"). It emerges when someone says "you can ONLY use three ingredients" and suddenly invents the perfect biscuit.

So maybe freedom isn't getting rid of all constraints. Maybe it's finding which constraints actually help your weird brain work better instead of worse.
