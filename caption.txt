There's this beautiful irony about vulnerability that most people miss until they've already screwed something up so spectacularly that there's nothing left but honesty.

We spend our entire lives building walls-metaphorical ones, obviously, unless you're literally an architect-and then we act surprised when those same walls become prisons. The moment someone tells you "just be vulnerable," your first instinct isn't trust. It's what if I'm wrong? Which, frankly, you probably will be. That's kind of the point.

Here's what nobody warns you: vulnerability doesn't feel like courage at first. It feels like incompetence. You put yourself out there-the idea, the feeling, the half-baked thesis you've been turning over since 3 AM-and the world looks back at you with the blank expression of someone who has absolutely no interest in your inner life. And for one horrible second, you think: this is exactly why I don't do this.

But here's the thing about failure as a teacher (yes, it teaches, everyone says that, bear with me). When you're willing to be bad at something-to let the raw, unpolished version of whatever you're making exist without apology-that's where actual originality hides. Not in perfection. Perfection is just fear wearing a nice outfit.
