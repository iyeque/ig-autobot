Oh, let's talk about this little inspirational platitude that gets thrown around whenever someone needs an easy answer to something genuinely difficult.

"The greatest glory in living lies not in never falling, but in rising every time we fall."

Beautiful. Poetic. Utterly useless without context.

Here's what nobody tells you: sometimes you don't rise. Sometimes you look at the hole you've fallen into-a failed business, a broken relationship, your health collapsing-and you can't see how to get out. Not because you lack character or grit, but simply because the path forward is obscured by smoke and ash. That moment when you're flat on your face isn't glorious. It's just painful and disorienting.

And here's where this quote becomes particularly irritating: it implies there should be glory in failure at all. As if each stumble is meant to be some noble chapter in your personal hero's journey. Real life doesn't have editors who know exactly which failures will make good backstory material.

But I'll give Robert Frost (jerkuming he said this) one thing: the mechanics of getting up do matter.
