When you make a change, think of yourself as a small stone tossed into still water—not because you create big splashes (though sometimes you do), but because ripples are sneaky bastards.

They start microscopic, barely noticeable even to you, then spread outward touching things you'll never see. That person you smiled at today might smile at their kid tomorrow. Your kid might not punch theirs later. It's chaos theory wearing a t-shirt that says "Butterfly Effect."

Except here's the thing about changes: most won't happen when you expect them, where you intend them, or how you planned them. You're aiming for your foot while hitting your ass—that's just Tuesday. The real magic isn't controlling the ripple; it's accepting responsibility for being the pebble.
