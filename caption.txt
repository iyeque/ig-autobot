Just like a shattered vase patched with gold, maybe our own cracks are the only places light can sneak in. I haven’t “mastered” this—still cloaking my dread of solitude in the buzzword “productivity.” But those fissures, as Leonard Cohen reminded us, aren’t flaws; they’re proof we survived the smash.

Kintsugi isn’t about restoring the original; it’s about turning ruin into something stranger, shinier, and undeniably ours. The business that went belly‑up, the relationship that imploded—yeah, they hurt. Yet now gold veins run through the seams, and honestly, a scar‑lined masterpiece beats a sterile, unblemished copy any day.
