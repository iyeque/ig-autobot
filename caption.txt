Right, let’s talk about this "starting over" business. It’s always a grand occasion, isn’t it? A fanfare of pristine intentions and a sudden, desperate need to build something… better. Frankly, it’s exhausting. Like trying to herd seagulls with a feather duster. You spend all this energy, meticulously planning every single detail, obsessing over the perfect script, the precise algorithm, the optimal colour palette... only to find that you've created a magnificent pile of dust bunnies.

Starting over is, in essence, the brutal truth that your initial attempts at creating something worthwhile are invariably going to be… profoundly mediocre.  It's a lesson hammered home by the relentless tide of human imperfection. We’re all fundamentally flawed, aren’t we?  And the universe doesn’t particularly care if your first attempt is a masterpiece or a regrettable puddle. It just wants to see what happens next. And usually, it seems to prefer a messy, slightly alarming mess.

There’s a certain exhilarating despair involved, I suppose.  The feeling of staring into the abyss and realizing that all those hours spent honing your skills - those meticulously crafted strategies - were ultimately for nothing.  You start to question everything.  What was I really trying to achieve? Was this even worth it?  The existential dread builds slowly, like a poorly-made soufflé, threatening to collapse entirely before you can salvage anything.

But here's the thing: it is teachable. It teaches us about productive failure.  It reveals the importance of embracing the ‘what ifs.
