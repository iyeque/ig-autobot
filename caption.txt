There's an old joke about how many angels can dance on the head of a pin that I've always found deeply offensive-not because it's stupid, but because it's almost profound. You take something vast (heaven), cram it into something absurdly small (a pinhead), and what do you get? A theological argument that makes people want to throw furniture at medieval scholars.

But here's where Wigman is smarter than those theologians: he doesn't claim the microcosm equals the macrocosm. He just asks you to look. And once you start looking-really looking-the whole metaphor collapses under its own weight in the most beautiful way possible.

Take your marriage. Your actual, living, breathing marriage-or if you're me, the concept of one. There's this tiny ritual my partner has when she folds laundry. She creases every shirt with military precision while watching reality TV, and there's something in that gesture that contains the entire history of human ambition: care so precise it borders on devotion, paired with entertainment so mindless it borders on nihilism. Two opposite impulses holding hands across a basket of socks. That's not just my relationship. That's every civilization ever built by creatures who simultaneously wanted to make things perfect...
