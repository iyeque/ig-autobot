We often hear stories that celebrate the “beautiful brokenness” of entrepreneurs, creators, or anyone who has faced hardship. While vulnerability is valuable, turning struggle into a badge can trap us in a cycle where pain is expected, even prized.

When we constantly glorify brokenness we:

Imply that success must be preceded by suffering.
Dismiss the very real need for rest, support, and healthy boundaries.
Make it harder for people to ask for help without feeling they’re “selling” their trauma.

Compassion means recognizing that hardship is a part of life, not a credential. It means offering space for recovery, acknowledging resilience without romanticising the wound, and allowing people to move forward without the weight of a narrative that demands they stay “broken” for validation.

Let’s shift the conversation: celebrate the moments of steadiness, the quiet decisions that keep us safe, and the ordinary acts of self‑care that sustain us. By doing so we create a culture where healing is seen as strength, not a story to be performed.
