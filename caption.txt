Imagine you’re at the rim of your life‑pond, that glassy surface that dutifully reflects every “you ought to” you’ve ever swallowed. Instead of a single pebble, you hurl nine.

That’s The Nine Stitches in a nutshell.

I set out to write a manual on “how not to fail” (because logic, right?). I ended up discovering that deliberately tripping over your own expectations is the fastest way to spot the rusted bolts holding you back.

The ripples are ugly. Your polished self‑portrait shatters. Strangers get splashed, too. Yet in that mess you finally ask: did I build a boat to weather storms, or just to look pretty docked?

“Embrace failure” isn’t the fluffy mantra on a coffee mug. It’s a forensic inspection—find the cracks before they sink you, then decide whether to patch them or scrap the hull entirely.
