Ah, yes-the magnificent art of almost.

You know those moments I'm talking about. You've just handed in your resignation letter and you're sitting at your desk for the last forty-five minutes because nobody told you what happens next. Your chair has been pushed back from the desk an inch-an act of symbolic rebellion that means absolutely nothing but feels enormous. That's between jobs. Between versions of yourself. And it is, against all reason, one of the most quietly beautiful things in life.

Here's what no productivity guru will tell you: the transition itself is the thing. Not the arrival. Not the destination. The liminal. The threshold. The awkward pause before the music starts again.

Think about how many of our best memories live right there-in the tremble between states. First love? Beautiful, sure-but the anticipation of first love, the way you'd rehearse conversations with someone while pretending to study, the electric misery of texting without sending-that was the masterpiece. The relationship was just the frame they hung it in.

Or consider grief, which is perhaps the most honest transitional space of all. You aren't who you were before loss, and you haven't yet become whoever you'll be after it. You're suspended. Raw-edged. Walking through rooms as if they belong to strangers. There's a terrible tenderness to that. A kind of grace dressed up as pain.
