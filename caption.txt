Trying to “fail” on purpose sounds like a paradox until you realize we spend more energy dodging disaster than mining its data.

When you set out knowing you’ll probably mess up, you’re not courting failure—you’re feeding the ego’s safety net. You’re actually training your tolerance for uncertainty, the breeding ground of every real growth spurt.

Productive failure is simple: treat each crash landing as a GPS ping, not a dead‑end. Your ambition shouldn’t shrink; it should widen to include a healthy curiosity about what happens when the plan goes sideways. Spoiler: it always does, and that’s where the juicy insights hide.
