Oh, darling, let’s talk about this. It’s one of my favorite little human delusions. The idea that our tiny, frantic lives matter in some grand, cosmic sense. That if we just recycle and smile at a barista, we’ll set off a chain reaction that ends with world peace and a perfectly chilled martini waiting for us in the great beyond. It’s adorable. Truly. Like watching a toddler try to build a sandcastle against an incoming tide.

We love this fantasy. We love it because it gives us a sense of control. In a universe so vast and indifferent it would swallow our sun without a burp, we cling to the notion that our choice of coffee mug is a stone dropped into the still waters of existence, sending out ripples that will eventually lap against the shores of… well, something important.

Here’s the inconvenient truth your self-help gurus won’t tell you, mostly because they’re busy trying to sell you a seminar on “Quantum Gratitude”: Most ripples don’t do junk.

Think about it. You hold the door open for someone. A small act of kindness! What’s the ripple? Maybe they feel slightly less irritated for three minutes. Maybe they, in turn, don’t slam their car door on the person parked next to them. Or maybe-and here’s where the real fun begins-maybe they were late for a meeting where they were going to deliver a crushing blow to a colleague’s career. By holding that door, you gave them the extra 45 seconds they needed to get there and ruin another life. Congratulations, you’ve just started a ripple of professional misery. Your good deed has become the butterfly flap that caused a hurricane in someone else’s soul.

Does this resonate?
