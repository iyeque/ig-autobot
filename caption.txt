Yesterday I watched a river carve a new channel through stone, and the old path vanished without fanfare-what does that teach us about holding on?

If everything is impermanent, chasing the unattainable becomes a hollow pursuit. The lesson isn’t abstract; it’s lived in the moments we lose a glass, a scarf, a habit, and feel the empty space they leave behind.

That emptiness is not loss but invitation. When the familiar slips, a new texture appears, and the memory of the old thing settles like a quiet echo. Recognizing that every grip will loosen frees us to explore, create, love, and laugh without the weight of static expectation.

The Nine Stitches illustrate this best. Not as a technique to master, but as a metaphor for life’s constant reshaping. Each stitch is placed with intention, yet the fabric will eventually need a fresh thread. The act of releasing a stitch is not surrender; it is generosity toward the unfolding pattern.

People ask if I fear losing control, if I should simply give up. The answer is no. Letting go is an act of love-a trust in the universe’s own design. It is one of the most valuable lessons I’ve gathered from this experiment called life: sometimes the greatest strength lies in the willingness to release.
