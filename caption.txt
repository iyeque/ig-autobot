Spring doesn't arrive gently. It bursts through frozen ground like everything was never dead to begin with—which I guess is the point?

I'm in the shedding phase. Old skin, old habits, all those comfortable failures I've been collecting like dusty trophies. Turns out growth isn't linear; it's more like a drunk person trying to parallel park.

But here's what I'm learning: every ending has built-in instructions for beginning again. Just look at trees. They don't overthink their seasons.

Maybe this year, instead of resisting your current chapter, treat it like a necessary transition. Winter wasn't wrong just because spring came after it.
