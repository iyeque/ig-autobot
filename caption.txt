Nothing lasts forever, so why do we act like our projects need to?

Change isn't a bug in your system—it's the operating system. That business plan you sweated over? Obsolete in five years. That relationship goal you've been grinding toward? Shifts into something else entirely while you weren't looking.

But here's where it gets interesting: Impermanence is actually the only thing guaranteeing new input. Without decay, there'd be no compost. Without endings, no blank pages. The Japanese concept of wabi-sabi doesn't just accept cracks—the whole philosophy celebrates them as proof that something lived.

So what opportunity lies within constant flux?

Everything. Every single day offers fresh material precisely because yesterday doesn't own today. Your failed startup becomes wisdom. Your broken heart becomes empathy. Your abandoned novel becomes practice for the next one.

Stop clinging to versions of yourself that expired last Tuesday. The real work isn't preserving anything—it's showing up curious enough to see what emerges from all this beautiful, terrifying mess.

Embrace the draft mode.
