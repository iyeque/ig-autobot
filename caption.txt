Let's talk failure—the thing everyone pretends to hug while secretly praying it never shows up on LinkedIn.

I spent years dissecting complex systems (read: things that love to implode spectacularly) and learned one truth: Failure isn’t a bug; it’s the operating system. You don’t “fix” it—you learn to dance with it.

Every catastrophic collapse teaches us what our successes conveniently forget. That bridge that fell? Thanks, next. Those financial models that vaporized retirees’ savings? Noted. My own spectacular career misfires? Very informative.

Designing systems that actually learn from failure means admitting your beautiful creation might be fundamentally flawed—a confession that triggers every ego‑defense mechanism humans invented after discovering fire.

So instead of building shinier hamster wheels for our organizations, ask: What would happen if we stopped treating failure as a moral sin and started seeing it as raw data?

Spoiler: it already is. Pretending otherwise just guarantees we repeat the same mistakes until someone writes a case study titled “How Preventable This Was.”

The Nine Stitches explores where physics meets philosophy when things fall apart beautifully. Read it if you enjoy cognitive dissonance and dark humor.
