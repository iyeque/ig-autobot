Oh, absolutely. Let's talk about rivers because they're magnificent metaphors for how we systematically destroy ourselves under the guise of "growth."

A river doesn't choose its course with wisdom and foresight. It flows where gravity tells it to go, crashes into obstacles, and either carves through them or gets dammed up entirely. We call this erosion. Nature calls it "Tuesday." And somehow, we've convinced ourselves that our own personal catastrophes-career implosions, relationship disasters, public humiliations-are also sculpting us into something more refined if only we have enough gratitude and perspective.

Here’s what I’ve learned watching water do exactly what physics demands: Resistance is beautiful but ultimately futile.

That boulder in the stream isn't being "tested" by the current. It's just getting worn down until it breaks apart or disappears completely. Your boss who gaslights you every Tuesday morning isn't teaching you resilience; he's eroding your self-worth like sediment. That toxic friendship that drains all your energy isn't building character; it's literally sapping away your life force while you stand there congratulating yourself for being such a good person.

The river has no ego. When it hits an immovable object, it doesn't declare itself defeated. It simply changes direction. You, however, will cling to failed strategies long past their expiration date because admitting defeat feels worse than continued suffering. The river knows nothing of pride. It floods. It dries up. It freezes solid. Then it tries again tomorrow without any guilt about yesterday's mistakes.

Have you experienced this?
