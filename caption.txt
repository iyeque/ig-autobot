The paradox of becoming through pretense. It's a notion that has long fascinated me, and one that is beautifully encapsulated in the epigraph: "To become, be calm. To be calm, pretend to be calm."

At first glance, this seems like a contradictory statement. How can we truly become who we are by pretending to be someone else? Isn't pretending just a form of deception, a facade that hides our true selves? But what if I told you that the line between authenticity and pretense is thin, and that sometimes, the act of pretending can be a powerful catalyst for becoming?

Think of it this way: when we pretend to be calm, we're not just putting on a mask; we're making a conscious choice to let go of our attachment to outcome. We're saying, "I don't need to achieve a specific state of being in order to be myself." And in that moment, we become free to simply be, without the burden of expectation.

This, I believe, is the essence of intention vs. outcome. When we focus on intention, we're not concerned with the end result; we're concerned with the process itself. We're concerned with becoming, not with achieving. And it's in this space of becoming that we find true calm, not because we've arrived at some predetermined destination, but because we've let go of our attachment to the destination altogether.
