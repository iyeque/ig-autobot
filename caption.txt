Of course we can't. And that's precisely why it's so goddamn fascinating.

We're creatures who've built civilizations out of things we refuse to drop-grudges older than our grandmothers, identities more fragile than spun sugar, relationships that stopped serving their purpose years ago but look good on paper (or at least, looked good once). We don't just hold onto shit; we tie ourselves in knots with it, then wonder why we can't move freely.

"Letting go" has become spiritualized into something you do while sipping overpriced tea, but here's the unvarnished truth: most of what holds us back isn't some mystical blockage requiring expensive retreats. It's just... inertia. Pride. Fear disguised as loyalty. The comfort of a known misery versus the terror of an unknown peace.

That question "can we?" is almost always answered by another one: do we want to?

There's a particular kind of bondage that feels like security because you know exactly where your chains touch skin. You could probably slip free if you wanted to badly enough-but wanting requires admitting that the cage was optional all along. That's the part that actually hurts. Not the cutting away of whatever weighs you down, but realizing you were carrying it willingly this whole time.

Freedom doesn't come from someone else releasing you. It comes from deciding that the weight isn't worth hauling anymore, even though you spent years convincing yourself it had value.

So yes, we can let go. But first, we have to stop treating our limitations like they're achievements.
